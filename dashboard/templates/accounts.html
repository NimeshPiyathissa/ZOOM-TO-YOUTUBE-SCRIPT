No password, 2-step code, cookie or token is ever stored, typed or transmitted by this system. An account is a label plus its own private Chrome profile directory on the VPS (0700, owned by the streaming user). You sign in yourself, by hand, in that Chrome window on the remote screen; afterwards the dashboard only asks Google whether a session exists. The session lives in the profile's cookies, survives Chrome and service restarts and reboots, and is what YouTube's player and Zoom's "Sign in with Google" use. Removing an account deletes the directory.

checking…

Sign in:

  1. A Chrome window has opened on the remote screen at Google's sign-in page, maximized. If you only see black, wait a few seconds.
  2. Sign in there exactly as on a phone: email, password, then any 2-Step prompt. Type into the remote screen, not this page - nothing you type is seen or kept by the dashboard.
  3. "This browser or app may not be secure" - Google's automation check. This window has no automation hooks (that is why sign-in never happens in the streaming kiosk, which is remote-controlled). What usually trips it is a new device on a datacenter IP: tap Try another way and approve on your phone, or use a backup code. If it still refuses, sign in to the same account once from your phone, then retry here within a few minutes.
  4. "Verify it's you" / device confirmation - expected on a first sign-in from this machine. Approve the prompt on your phone or enter the code; that confirmation is remembered in this profile.
  5. "Couldn't sign you in" - a wrong password, a suspended account, or Google blocking the IP for now. Check the account from your phone; if it works there, wait 10-15 minutes and retry here.
  6. Session expired later (the account shows needs re-authentication): Google ends sessions after inactivity or a password change. Tap Re-authenticate - same window, same steps.
  7. When you can see your account avatar top-right on Google, tap I'm done. The window closes so the session is written to disk, then the dashboard loads a signed-in-only Google page in that profile and reads the account identity - it never assumes success.
